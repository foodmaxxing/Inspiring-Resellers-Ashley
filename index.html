The Creators Who Got Me Into Reselling

This is the first in a series of posts about the people whose content actually influenced me. Not a hall of fame thing, just honest credit where it's due.

cadmusfinds  ·  Reselling & Side Hustle

I didn't stumble into reselling on my own. I had help.

At first, a good friend of mine introduced me to his Garage Sale hobby. My friend, let's call him Sam, called Saturday mornings his own, away from family to drive to as many Garage Sales as possible and find valuable items for cheap to flip on eBay. His journey started at 7:00 AM and he was back home by 12:00 PM! That's it. One day, he invited me to ride with him and learn one or two things so I could join him in the hobby. I was hooked :)

We won't be talking about my friend Sam today since he's a private citizen and not a public figure in any way, but I thought telling this story without honorably mentioning him is completely wrong!

But unlike my disciplined friend (who we won't be talking about anymore :P) who only sourced one day of the week and only from Garage Sales, I had to go HARDER. Going harder was thanks to a few YouTube creators who were already out there doing it and just... showing people how it worked.

This is the first in a series of posts about the people whose content actually influenced me. Not a hall of fame thing, just honest credit where it's due. Ashley Nicholes, Hustle at Home Mom, is a good place to start.

How I Even Found This World

Doubling down on the reselling world that I was just exposed to meant spending countless hours watching YouTube videos and going down many rabbit holes. And somewhere in that spiral I landed on reselling content of people going to thrift stores, finding things other people didn't recognize the value of, and selling them online for a profit.

I watched a few videos and something just clicked. I don't have to limit myself to Saturday morning occasional Garage Sales, there are more interesting ways to source. The YouTubers I watched treated this fun "hobby" as an actual business with a system, a sourcing strategy, and real numbers. Ashley was one of the first creators I found, and one of the ones that stuck.

What Hustle at Home Mom Is Actually About

Ashley Nicholes is a stay-at-home mom who built her reselling business from the ground up. She documents the whole thing on YouTube. The sourcing trips, the hauls, what sells, what doesn't, how she thinks about pricing, even tutorials on how to clean and repair clothing items that are slightly damaged, stained, or flawed. Her content is real!

Her eBay store is called Clean and Crisp, and it's exactly what it sounds like. She focuses on quality clothing, things that are clean and in great shape, sourced from thrift stores and sold to people who want a deal on something nice.

Watching her work made me realize that clothing reselling is actually a skill. You have to know brands. You have to know condition. You have to know what people are actually searching for and willing to pay for. It's not just grabbing anything off a rack.

The Goodwill Bins Changed Everything

Here's where it got interesting for me. The Goodwill Outlet, AKA the bins, is a whole different animal from a regular thrift store. Instead of items hanging on racks with price tags, everything gets dumped into giant bins and you pay by the pound. It's chaotic. It's crowded. And if you know what you're doing, it can be incredibly profitable because your cost per item is so low.

Watching creators like Ashley dig through bins and pull out name-brand clothing for literal cents on the dollar was honestly kind of mind-blowing the first time I saw it. I had never been to a Goodwill Outlet before and didn't know a place like this even existed.

Once you know a few brands to look for, you can't un-know them. You start moving through bins with a purpose instead of just poking around. That's the treasure hunt everyone talks about, and it's real. Finding something good in a bin feels way better than it probably should.

She Was Part of Why I Actually Started

Ashley wasn't the only creator whose content pushed me to get off the couch and go source something. There were a few. But she was one of them, and she deserves the credit.

I already had some instincts in certain categories like cameras and electronics mostly. The clothing angle was something I wasn't really tapping into. Her content made me take it more seriously. And the bins specifically became part of how I source because I saw how well it worked for people who knew what they were doing.

I started slow. Listed some stuff I already had. Made a few sales. Then started going to the bins with a list of brands in my head and actually finding things. It's a grind but it's a fun grind, which makes a difference.

Why I Also Do Affiliate Marketing Now

Real talk, reselling has a physical ceiling. You need space. You need time to source, photograph, list, pack, and ship. Life circumstances change, and mine did. Storage became a real constraint and I needed something that could run leaner. I also want to keep my wife happy, and let me tell you, she has not been a fan of my crowded office space and products on garage shelving.

Affiliate marketing filled that gap. The idea is simple: share links to products you'd actually recommend, and if someone buys through your link you earn a small commission. No inventory. No shipping. Just content and links.

It's not a complete replacement for the eBay side of things for now since I still run my own store, but it works alongside it in a way that makes sense for where I'm at right now. And yes, full transparency: this article is part of that. More on that in a second.
