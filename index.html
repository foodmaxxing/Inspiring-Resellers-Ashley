The Creators Who Got Me Into Reselling Starting With Hustle at Home Mom

A personal look at the YouTube creators who introduced me to reselling, starting with Ashley Nicholes from Hustle at Home Mom.

I did not stumble into reselling on my own. I had help.

At first, a good friend of mine introduced me to his garage sale hobby. My friend, let’s call him Sam, would leave early on Saturdays, hit as many garage sales as possible, and flip items on eBay. He would start at 7:00 AM and be done by noon.

One day, he invited me along. I was hooked.

We will not go deep into Sam’s story since he is a private person, but I had to mention him because he was part of the beginning.

Unlike Sam, I ended up going much deeper into the reselling world.

How I Found This World

Doubling down on this new world meant spending hours on YouTube learning how people actually sourced inventory.

I found creators going into thrift stores, pulling overlooked items, and selling them for profit online.

That is when it clicked. I did not need to rely only on garage sales. There were many ways to source inventory.

Creators treated reselling like a real business with systems and strategy. Ashley Nicholes was one of the first I found.

What Hustle at Home Mom Is About

Ashley Nicholes built a reselling business from scratch while being a stay at home mom. She documents everything on YouTube including sourcing, hauls, pricing decisions, and even cleaning tutorials for damaged clothing.

Her eBay store is called Clean and Crisp. It focuses on quality thrifted clothing in good condition.

She shows that reselling clothing is a skill. You need to understand brands, condition, and demand.

The Goodwill Bins Changed Everything

The Goodwill Outlet, also called the bins, is different from a normal thrift store. Items are dumped into large bins and sold by weight.

It is chaotic, crowded, and extremely cheap if you know what you are doing.

Seeing creators pull high value items for pennies changed how I thought about sourcing.

Once you learn brands, you cannot unlearn them.

The bins feel like a treasure hunt. Finding something valuable in chaos is part of what makes it addictive.

Why She Influenced Me

Ashley was not the only creator, but she was one of the most influential for me personally.

I already had some experience with electronics and cameras, but clothing was new to me.

Her content pushed me to take it seriously.

I started listing items, making sales, and eventually sourcing with intention at the bins.

Why I Also Do Affiliate Marketing

Reselling has a physical limit. You need space, time, and energy to manage inventory.

Life changed for me and storage became an issue. I also needed something more flexible.

Affiliate marketing allows me to recommend products and earn small commissions without holding inventory.

It works alongside reselling, not as a replacement.
